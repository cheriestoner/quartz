## Appendix
My presentation:

**SLIDE 1**
Hello, my name is Xuehua Fu, and I am a PhD student at University Jean Monnet in Saint-Etienne, France

My project is titled _‘Seeing Voices – Sensory Substitution for Vocal Communication’_.

My supervisors are Dr. Katarzyna Pisanski and Prof. Dr. David Reby, and we are all working in the ENES bioacoustics lab at University Jean Monnet.

The main goal of this project is to explore how vocal information can be translated into another sensory modality—specifically vision—to support communication in contexts where hearing is limited or absent. More specifically, we aim to develop and validate visual spectrographic representations that allow deaf and hard of hearing users to “see” functionally and emotionally relevant information in the human voice.

**SLIDE 2 **

A bit of my background: my bachelor’s studies were in comp maths, and then I completed an MSc in Computer Science, at KTH Royal Institute of Technology. I specialized in visualization, music acoustics, and human-computer interaction, and developed skills in both perceptual and computational studies.

The research plan revolves around the core questions of how people can read vocal information from spectrograms, and how spectrograms can be enhanced using crossmodal correspondences to make vocal information more intuitive and perceptually accessible.

In terms of execution, the plan follows five stages, including perceptual and computational modeling of vocal and crossmodal perception, followed by design and prototyping, the development of real-time interactive spectrogram-based systems, and finally the exploration of real-world applications—particularly to support Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing users.

So far we have started dataset of nonverbal, for conducting psychoacoustic experiments and for machine learning analysis 

Regarding training, I will be participating in doctoral courses, VoCS network training events, and cross-disciplinary methodological training. For example, at the ENES bioacoustics lab, I will receive supervised training in conducting psychoacoustics experiments with human subjects. 

For the secondments, two placements are planned: one at VoCoLab at University College London with Prof. Carolyn McGettingan, and one at Cochlear Inc. The exact dates and duration are still to be determined, but these secondments will allow me to investigate the neural aspects of crossmodal perception and applications in assistive technologies.
