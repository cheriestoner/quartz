# Objectives
_This is a formative, generative study. The prototype is not being evaluated. The goal is to produce hypotheses, design insights, and a tested protocol._
### 1. Explore embodied and sensorimotor engagement
To explore how dynamic spectrogram visualisation may engage sensorimotor and embodied processes in voice perception — both in relation to one’s own voice and in the perception of others’ voices — and to generate hypotheses about whether it can substitute for or augment auditory motor simulation.
### 2. Inform future interface design
To identify design opportunities for the live spectrogram interface that better support sensorimotor and embodied processes in voice perception, based on participant feedback and observed behaviour.
### 3. Pilot the behavioural experiment protocol
To test the feasibility and clarity of a three-condition reconstruction task for use in a subsequent online or in-person behavioural experiment.
# Participants

|~5 experts (acousticians)|Experienced spectrogram readers — reveals whether effects depend on learned visual-acoustic mapping|

# Protocol

## General approach
- Semi-structured interview follows each task block
- Researcher follows emergent responses

_Think-aloud (verbalising during performing the task) was intentionally excluded._
## Task 1 — Vocalisation with real-time visual feedback
Participants vocalise freely while observing live spectrogram feedback of their own voice. The goal is to explore the self-voice sensorimotor loop: does seeing one’s voice alter the felt experience of vocalising?

Suggested vocalisations (encourage free exploration too):
- Nonverbal vocalisations: laugh, cry, scream, moan, roar, etc.
- Sustained vowels
- Singing
- Speech

Interview questions about their user experience follows.
## Task 2 — Vocal reconstruction (three conditions)
Participants reconstruct a target nonverbal vocalisation (from a curated stimuli set?) under three conditions.
### Condition A — Audio only
Target sound played without spectrogram. Participant reconstructs with their voice.
### Condition B — Visual only
Animated spectrogram displayed without audio. Participant reconstructs the sound having never heard it.

_Theoretically central condition. Tests whether dynamic visual representation can activate the motor simulation pathway that audio normally drives._
### Condition C — Audio + visual
Both animated spectrogram and audio presented simultaneously. Participant reconstructs.

After each condition, participants listen back to their own reconstruction alongside the target, then move to interview questions.
# Interview Questions
## After Task 1
- What did you notice about your voice while watching the spectrogram?
- Did seeing the feedback change how you vocalised, or how it felt?
- Any moments where you felt the visualisation helped or interfered?
- Was the visualisation informative, distracting, or something else?
## After Task 2 (each condition)
- How did you approach the reconstruction? What were you paying attention to?
- Did condition B (visual only) feel possible, impossible, or something in between?
- Was there a felt difference between the three conditions — in difficulty, in experience, in how ‘present’ the sound felt?
- Did the spectrogram feel like something to decode, or did it suggest movement, gesture, something more direct?
## General / closing
- Was there anything about the interface that made you want to explore further?
- What would make the visualisation more useful or more engaging?
- Any moments where the visual and auditory felt connected — or disconnected?
# Acoustic Observation
Reconstructions will be recorded and informally analysed (descriptively, not statistically). The purpose is feasibility assessment not hypothesis testing.

Observations of interest:
- Does condition B produce any acoustic resemblance to the target (pitch contour, duration, spectral character)?
- Is there a perceptible difference between conditions A and B reconstructions?
- Do expert and naive participants differ in reconstruction quality across conditions?
# Open Questions for Follow-up Design

- Which features of the dynamic spectrogram do participants actually respond to — motion, colour, rhythm, shape?
- Does the expert/naive distinction reflect learned decoding or more direct motor resonance?
- Which stimulus type produces the clearest embodied response?
- Is the vocalisation reconstruction task with three conditions feasible to be scaled up into a behavioural experiment, or does it need redesign?
