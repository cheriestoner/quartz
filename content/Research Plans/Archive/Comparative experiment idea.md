**A Comparative Experiment on Crossmodal Perception of Vocal Spectrograms**

**Background & Motivation**
Inspired by the Beyond Speech paper:
- Speech tends to be low-pitched, tonal, and produced with regular phonation. Its communicative power relies heavily on supralaryngeal articulatory modulation (vocal filter).
- Singing and especially nonverbal vocalizations involve greater pitch variation, roughness, and irregular phonation, relying more on vocal source modulation.
- The paper inferred that source modulation conveys affect most effectively, while filter modulation supports semantic communication.

**Question**
Are these acoustic differences visually perceivable through spectrograms, especially by untrained viewers?

**Research Goal**
Investigate how people interpret spectrograms of speech, nonverbal vocalizations, and singing, and whether they rely on different visual cues for each category.

**Proposed Experiment**
1. Categorization Task
	Test whether untrained participants can distinguish speech/nonverbal vocalizations/singing purely by looking at spectrograms.
2. Visual Attention Analysis
	- Measure **which visual regions or patterns** participants attend to when interpreting spectrograms of each vocal type.
	- Methods: **eye-tracking** (primary), optionally **EEG** for exploratory neural correlates.
    - Hypothesis:
	    - Speech → attention to **low-frequency, formant-like structures**
	    - Nonverbal vocalizations → attention to **roughness patterns, NLP**
	    - Singing → attention to **pitch** and **harmonic structure**

**Outcome**
This experiment can reveal how acoustic differences in vocal production translate into **visual perceptual strategies**, and inform the design of future voice-visualization interfaces.
